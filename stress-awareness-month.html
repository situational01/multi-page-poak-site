Stress Awareness Month: You Are Not Alone

April 5, 2026 by POAK Mental Health Team 5 min read

April is Stress Awareness Month – a time dedicated to recognising the impact of stress on our lives, breaking the silence, and reminding each other that we are not alone. In Kenya, especially in informal settlements like Kibera, Mathare, and Kiambiu, stress is often a hidden burden. But it doesn't have to be.

What is Stress Awareness Month?

Stress Awareness Month has been observed every April since 1992. Its goal is to increase public understanding of the causes and cures of stress, and to encourage open, honest conversations about mental well‑being. This year, the theme is “#NotAlone” – because sharing your struggles is the first step toward healing.

Why This Matters for Our Communities

Living in poverty comes with daily stressors: lack of stable income, overcrowded housing, food insecurity, and limited access to healthcare. For many, these pressures never stop. According to the Kenya Mental Health Taskforce Report, one in four Kenyans experiences a mental health condition, yet stigma prevents most from seeking help.

Unmanaged stress can lead to depression, anxiety, substance abuse, and even physical illness. But when we talk about stress openly, we reduce shame, build resilience, and create pathways to support.

“I thought stress was a sign of weakness. I never told anyone how heavy my chest felt. Then I joined a POAK support group and realised others felt the same. Now I know – I am not alone.”

- Anonymous

Common Signs of Stress – Listen to Your Body

Stress affects us physically, emotionally, and socially. Look out for:

  • Persistent headaches, back pain, or muscle tension
  • Changes in sleep – either too much or too little
  • Feeling irritable, angry, or overwhelmed by small things
  • Loss of appetite or overeating
  • Withdrawing from friends, family, or community activities
  • Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
  • Using alcohol, cigarettes, or drugs to cope

If you recognise any of these signs, it is not a personal failure. It is your body telling you that you need support.

Start the Conversation – It’s Safe to Talk

One of the most powerful tools against stress is a simple conversation. Here’s how you can start:

  1. Choose someone you trust – a friend, family member, community health worker, or a POAK support group facilitator.
  2. Start small – “I’ve been feeling really tired lately, and I’m not sure why.”
  3. Listen without judgement – when someone shares with you, just listen. You don’t need to solve everything.
  4. Remind them (and yourself) – “You are not alone. Many people feel this way.”

Practical Ways to Manage Stress – Low Cost, High Impact

You don’t need expensive therapy to begin managing stress. Try these free or low‑cost techniques:

  • Deep breathing – Inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4, exhale for 4. Repeat 5‑10 times.
  • Move your body – A 15‑minute walk or simple stretches release endorphins.
  • Talk to someone – Isolation makes stress worse. Even a short chat helps.
  • Create a small routine – Wake up and sleep at roughly the same time each day.
  • Limit bad news – Too much negative information fuels anxiety. Take breaks from social media.

How POAK Can Help You

Our Mental Health & Wellness Program offers free, confidential support in Kibera, Mathare, Kawangware, and other informal settlements. We provide:

  • Weekly support groups where you can share and listen in a safe space
  • Individual counselling sessions with trained staff
  • Stress management workshops and relaxation techniques
  • Referrals to specialised care when needed

You do not need a formal referral. Just walk into any POAK community centre or call us on +254 708 270 533 to find a group near you.
